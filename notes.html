How it works

From raw readings to a training record

01

Readings come in

Tags on the bar and plates are read many times a second while you lift. Nothing to wear, nothing to press.

02

The model finds the lift

Our software turns those readings into the bar's movement and finds each rep in it.

03

The record writes itself

Sets, reps and bar speed go straight into your log. You confirm with one tap when it isn't sure.

Datum is the software layer. It runs on standard RFID readers and tags, and is being built to take other inputs too, such as heart rate from a watch or strap.

The research

Why bar speed matters

These studies used dedicated velocity devices and their own participants, not Datum Strength. They explain why we're building it, not how well it works.

0.98

Bar speed tracks how heavy a lift is

In 120 trained men, bar speed and percent of max were almost perfectly related, and stayed stable after a 9.3% strength gain.

González-Badillo and Sánchez-Medina, International Journal of Sports Medicine, 2010
40%

Fewer reps, same strength

Stopping squat sets at a 20% speed drop gave similar strength gains to a 40% drop, with 40% fewer reps.

Pareja-Blanco et al., Scandinavian Journal of Medicine and Science in Sports, 2017
0.58

Feedback keeps the bar moving

Adolescent athletes who saw their bar speed held 0.58 m/s on rep 10 of a squat set, against 0.49 m/s without it.

Weakley et al., Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 2019
3

Three ways coaches use velocity

Estimating a max without testing it, adjusting load day to day, and capping how much speed a set can lose.

Guppy, Kendall and Haff, Strength and Conditioning Journal, 2023

Questions

Straight answers

Does it use cameras?

No. It reads small radio tags on your equipment. Nothing in your gym is filmed.

Do I have to wear anything?

No. The tags are on the equipment, not on you. Heart rate from a watch or chest strap is planned as an option.

Will it work with my equipment?

It's built for the bars, plates, dumbbells and kettlebells you already own. The tags stick on, so there is nothing to replace.

How accurate is it?

On our Nov 21 garage session its rep counts matched the written log on all 29 reps. That session was also used to build the method, so independent tests come next. Bar speed and depth are measured but not yet checked against a dedicated device. We'll publish those comparisons.

Can I buy one?

Not yet. The first kits go to a small group of home gyms. Join the waitlist to be considered.

What will it cost?

Pricing isn't final. The plan is a yearly software license, with tags and hardware sold at about cost.
